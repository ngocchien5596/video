# Risks that limit reach for AI-assisted animated Shorts and TikToks (YouTube / TikTok, 2025–2026): policy, labels, cross-posting, Vietnam law

Legend: **[OFFICIAL]** = platform or government page/newsroom; **[3P]** = press, law firm or blog; **[INFERENCE]** = my reasoning, not stated by any source. Research date: 2026-11-10. Help pages were fetched live, so they show current wording.

## 1. AI disclosure labels (YouTube "altered or synthetic", TikTok AIGC label, C2PA)

### Takeaway
On YouTube, a fully animated, clearly non-realistic video does NOT need the altered/synthetic disclosure. YouTube says officially that disclosing "alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." TikTok requires a label only for *realistic* AI scenes or people. It auto-labels through C2PA Content Credentials and its own detection, and it has not said that labels reduce distribution. It does now let viewers choose to see *less* AIGC in their feed.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube: "We require creators to disclose AI-generated or altered content that seems realistic." This covers a real person saying or doing something they didn't, altered footage of real events or places, a realistic scene that never happened, and AI music that is the main focus of the video. — [YouTube Help: Disclosing altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube: disclosure is not required for clearly unrealistic or animated content (examples: a unicorn ride, an AI-animated missile in a fully animated video), for minor aesthetic edits, for production help (AI-drafted scripts, outlines, titles, thumbnails, captions, ideas), or for audio and video repair or upscaling. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube: for non-music AI audio or non-photorealistic/animated AI content, the label may appear in the expanded description rather than on the player. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube: "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube, on not labeling: "Creators who consistently choose not to disclose this information may be subject to manual application of a label," and may face "penalties from YouTube, including removal of content or suspension from the YouTube Partner Program." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- [OFFICIAL] TikTok: "we require creators to label AI-generated or significantly edited content that shows realistic-looking scenes or people"; "Unlabeled content may be removed, restricted, or labeled by our team, depending on the harm it could cause." Misleading AIGC on matters of public importance is banned. Misinformation that may cause moderate harm is FYF-ineligible. — [TikTok Community Guidelines: Integrity & Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- [OFFICIAL] TikTok (Nov 2025 newsroom): labels come from creator labels, TikTok detection models and C2PA Content Credentials, and labels now show which of these was the source. TikTok is testing "invisible watermarking" ("a robust technological 'watermark' that only we can read") on content made with TikTok AI tools (e.g., AI Editor Pro) and on uploads that carry C2PA. Over 1.3 billion videos have been labeled. It is also testing an AIGC control in "Manage topics" so users can choose how much AIGC they see in For You, "rather than removing or replacing content in feeds entirely." The post says nothing about labels affecting ranking. — [TikTok Newsroom, Nov 2025](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-content?lang=en); also [TikTok Newsroom SSA, Mar 2026](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-ssa-shares-more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-generated-content?lang=en-ZA)
- [3P] TikTok began auto-labeling AI content from other platforms through C2PA in May 2024. — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2024/05/09/tiktok-transparency-labels-for-generative-ai-content/), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/05/10/tiktok_ai_watermarks/)
- [3P, unverified] Claim that an unlabeled video flagged as synthetic is "instantly disqualified" from Creator Rewards. This is a single blog and was not confirmed by TikTok. — via [lilachbullock.com](https://www.lilachbullock.com/ai-generated-videos-monetised-tiktok/)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] For a stylized animated kids' science series, YouTube disclosure is generally not required. Toggling it anyway has no stated reach penalty. On TikTok, assets from AI tools that embed C2PA (many image and video generators do) may be auto-labeled "AI-generated" whatever the creator does. Toggling the label yourself avoids the "unlabeled" enforcement path.
- [INFERENCE] The new TikTok AIGC preference control means some viewers who choose to see less AI will see less AI-labeled content. This is an indirect reach effect driven by users, not a platform penalty.

### Gaps
- Neither platform publishes data on whether labeled videos get fewer views. TikTok has no explicit official statement that "labels don't affect FYF."
- YouTube's handling of C2PA metadata on upload (the "captured with a camera" label) was not researched in depth.

## 2. YouTube "inauthentic content" (July 15, 2025; formerly "repetitious content")

### Takeaway
This is a **YPP monetization policy**. The official page describes only monetization consequences, not recommendation penalties. It targets channels where "content feels interchangeable from video to video," including "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production." A templated daily series is allowed if each episode has a distinct storyline or concept. Shared intros and outros are fine.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] The July 15, 2025 update renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" and clarified that it covers content that is "repetitive or mass-produced." — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [OFFICIAL] "Generic or repetitive content includes content that looks like it's made with a template"; "channels where content feels interchangeable from video to video are not allowed to monetize." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [OFFICIAL] Not allowed: image slideshows, templated storylines or scrolling text with minimal narrative or educational value; repeating the same situation with the same outcome; "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [OFFICIAL] Allowed: shared intros and outros if most of the content differs; series channels where each video has a distinct storyline, focus or concept. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [OFFICIAL] Reused content: allowed "if viewers can tell that there's a meaningful difference"; the policy applies "even if you have permission" and "applies to your channel as a whole." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [OFFICIAL] Consequences: loss of monetization, limited or no ads, and YPP suspension or termination for serious or repeated violations. The page says nothing about recommendations. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [3P, reporting YouTube Liaison Rene Ritchie] He called it a "minor update": "This kind of content has been ineligible for monetization for years, and it's content that viewers often consider spam." He said AI tools are welcome (citing autodubbing and Dream Screen) and that reaction or clip content is unaffected. His examples of violations were narrative-story channels with only superficial differences and slideshow channels with the same narration. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550123/), [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/youtube-clarifies-monetisation-policy-targeting-inauthentic-content-9493155), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/)
- [3P] Some press framed it as a crackdown on "AI slop," with harsher implications than YouTube's "minor" framing. — [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/youtube-ramps-up-campaign-against-ai-slop-with-stricter-monetization-caveats-on-inauthentic-and-repetitive-videos)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] AI voices are not banned per se. The risk factor is *interchangeability*. A daily series with original scripts is lower risk if each episode answers a distinct question and has its own visuals and arc, rather than the same template with swapped keywords. Same voice, same structure, same thumbnail and thin content is the risky pattern.
- [INFERENCE] Reach suppression is not stated, but content viewers perceive as spammy tends to underperform on satisfaction signals anyway. Treat the recommendation effect as plausible but unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- No official statement on whether the inauthentic-content policy affects recommendations or Shorts feed distribution.

## 3. TikTok: unoriginal content, AI content, Creator Rewards Program (CRP)

### Takeaway
Officially, unoriginal or reused material "without anything new" is **ineligible for the For You feed**. That is a direct reach limit, unlike YouTube's monetization-only rule. CRP rewards original content over 1 minute and scores "originality" as a core metric. AI content is not banned, but realistic AI must be labeled.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] "Content is also ineligible for the FYF if it includes unoriginal or reused material without anything new." Content that violates IP rights is removed. — [TikTok Community Guidelines: Integrity & Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- [OFFICIAL] "If a video isn't getting many views, it also doesn't necessarily mean it broke a rule." FYF standards also limit content "not suitable for a broad audience." — [TikTok FYF standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards)
- [3P quoting TikTok Newsroom] CRP "rewards high-quality, original content that is over a minute long ... focused on four core metrics: originality, play duration, search value and audience engagement." — via [tabcut.com](https://www.tabcut.com/blog/post/the-new-tiktok-creator-rewards-program-everything-you-need-to-know)
- [3P] Eligibility commonly cited as 18+, a personal account, 10,000 followers and 100,000 views in the last 30 days. Duets, Stitches and minimally edited clips don't qualify, and unoriginal content can lead to program suspension. — [nuelink](https://blog.nuelink.com/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/), [tabcut](https://www.tabcut.com/blog/post/the-new-tiktok-creator-rewards-program-everything-you-need-to-know)
- [3P] Creators report viral videos being disqualified from CRP before payout. — [techissuestoday](https://techissuestoday.com/tiktok-creators-cry-foul-as-viral-videos-get-disqualified-before-payday)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Short (under 60 s) Shorts-style clips earn nothing from CRP. A CRP-oriented TikTok version would need to run over 1 minute.
- [INFERENCE] CRP's availability in Vietnam was not verified. CRP is limited to certain countries, so it may not apply to a Vietnam-based account.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch TikTok's official CRP page directly. The country list, including Vietnam, is unverified.

## 4. "Made for kids" (YouTube / COPPA) and TikTok kid-directed handling

### Takeaway
Setting a YouTube video as Made for Kids (MFK) officially disables comments, notifications, miniplayer, end screens and cards, save to playlist and Watch Later, personalized ads, autoplay on home and more. YouTube says MFK videos are "more likely to be recommended alongside other kids' videos." The creator must set the audience honestly, and mislabeling carries platform and COPPA legal consequences. A science channel aimed at general audiences, families or teens, rather than at under-13s, can legitimately be "not made for kids." The decision depends on intended audience and style, not on the topic.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Not available on MFK watch pages: autoplay on home, cards or end screens, video watermarks, channel memberships, comments, donate button, live chat, merch and ticketing, notifications, personalized advertising, playback in the Miniplayer, Super Chat/Stickers, "Save to playlist and Save to Watch Later." Not available on MFK channels: memberships, notifications, posts, comments. — [YouTube Help: Features unavailable on made for kids content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9632097)
- [OFFICIAL] "Videos that you set as 'made for kids' are more likely to be recommended alongside other kids' videos." Factors include subject matter, child actors, characters or toys appealing to kids, language and activities aimed at children, and advertising to children. "Do not rely on our systems to set your audience for you"; "If you fail to categorize your content correctly, there may be consequences on YouTube," plus possible legal consequences under COPPA. — [YouTube Help: Determining if content is made for kids](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076)
- [OFFICIAL] Kids and family quality principles determine YouTube Kids inclusion and recommendations, and "high-quality" MFK content is boosted. Low-quality signals: "deceptively educational" (clickbait that doesn't teach, or wrong information), sensational or misleading content, encouraging dangerous pranks or negative behaviors, "hard to follow" mass-produced or autogenerated content, heavily promotional content, and strange use of kids' characters. A channel focused on low-quality MFK content may be suspended from YPP, and violating videos may get limited or no ads. — [YouTube Help: Best practices for kids & family content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223)
- [3P] MFK content is excluded from personalized recommendations for signed-in adults; MFK RPM is often far lower (uncited claims of 40–70% lower). — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/youtube-hacks/coppa-20-and-gdpr-k-what-kids-creators-must-know-in-2026), [wildandfreetools](https://wildandfreetools.com/blog/youtube-watch-time-kids-channels-coppa/) (low reliability)
- [3P/background] YouTube began treating MFK content under COPPA on Jan 6, 2020, after the FTC settlement. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtubes-coppa-changes-begin-today-possibly-affecting-creator-revenue/342433/); academic measurement in [MSI working paper "COPPAcalypse"](https://www.msi.org/working-paper/coppacalypse-the-youtube-settlements-impact-on-kids-content/)
- [3P, citing a TikTok document submitted to the U.S. Senate] TikTok's minimum age is 13. U.S. users under 13 go to "TikTok for Younger Users," a curated view-only experience with no posting, comments, messages or ads. TikTok deletes under-13 accounts found outside it. Accounts for ages 13–15 are private by default. DMs and LIVE are limited to 16+. — [Senate document](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/index.php/services/files/8C751FF4-A1FD-4FCA-80F6-C84BEB04C2F9), [TechCrunch 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/13/tiktok-update-will-change-privacy-settings-and-defaults-for-users-under-18/)
- [3P, unverified] "Content from users under 16 is not allowed in the FYP." — [icuc.social](https://www.icuc.social/resources/blog/tiktok-content-moderation)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] With MFK on, Shorts loses comments and the notification bell, and recommendations shift toward kids' inventory. That likely lowers reach among adults and teens, and revenue falls because only contextual ads run. If the real target is families, teens or curious adults, styling and stating the series as general audience is defensible. If it is clearly aimed at small children (nursery style, characters for toddlers), it must be MFK. Misclassifying to avoid MFK limits is a COPPA risk.
- [INFERENCE] TikTok has no "made for kids" toggle. Content aimed at under-13s doesn't fit TikTok's 13+ user base, so frame TikTok content for 13+.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube statement on how MFK specifically changes Shorts-feed distribution, and no official data on view impact.
- YouTube Kids app eligibility criteria beyond the quality principles (e.g., whether Shorts appear in YouTube Kids) were not confirmed.

## 5. Cross-posting and watermarks

### Takeaway
No official YouTube source was found saying YouTube demotes Shorts with TikTok watermarks. The claim circulates only on marketing blogs. Instagram officially said it would not promote Reels with other platforms' watermarks. TikTok makes "unoriginal or reused material" FYF-ineligible. Best practice is to upload the clean original file to each platform.

### Cited Findings
- [3P reporting YouTube] In 2022 YouTube began adding a Shorts watermark to Shorts downloaded from Studio, so cross-posted clips trace back to YouTube. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/18/youtube-shorts-will-start-adding-watermarks-to-discourage-cross-platform-sharing/amp/), [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-will-now-automatically-add-watermarks-to-downloaded-shorts-clips/629940)
- [3P, unverified] "YouTube has publicly stated Shorts with visible watermarks from other platforms may receive reduced recommendations." This claim is from a tool vendor, with no official source located. — [goshorts blog](https://goshorts-marketing.pages.dev/blog/remove-tiktok-watermark-youtube-shorts.html)
- [OFFICIAL, TikTok] Unoriginal or reused material without anything new is FYF-ineligible. — [TikTok CG](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- [OFFICIAL, YouTube] Reused-content (monetization) rules target content repurposed from other online sources. Posting your own original video on both platforms is not "reused" in the sense of someone else's content, but the policy applies "even if you have permission." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Export one clean master (no platform logos), upload it natively to each platform, and add platform-native titles and captions. Posting the same video on both at the same time carries no documented penalty, because each platform judges originality within its own ecosystem. A visible TikTok logo on YouTube, or a Shorts logo on TikTok, plausibly signals "reposted," so avoid it.
- [INFERENCE] TikTok's C2PA reading means re-encoding doesn't matter for originality. Keep metadata consistent if you want honest AI labeling.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube statement (2025–2026) on other-platform watermarks.

## 6. Community Guidelines risks for kids' science content

### Takeaway
The main risks are home-experiment imitation (fire, chemicals, electricity), scientific or health misinformation, and anything that sexualizes or endangers minors. YouTube's kids quality rules also penalize "deceptively educational" clickbait and encouraging unsafe behavior. TikTok makes moderate-harm misinformation FYF-ineligible.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube's kids low-quality principles include "encouraging negative behaviors" (dangerous or unsafe pranks) and "deceptively educational" content showing wrong information. These lead to limited ads, lower recommendations or YPP suspension. — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223)
- [OFFICIAL] TikTok: "Content is ineligible for the FYF if it contains misinformation that may cause moderate harm"; content under fact-checker review is also FYF-ineligible. — [TikTok CG Integrity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- [OFFICIAL] TikTok prohibits youth abuse and exploitation. Lower-severity degrading content can be FYF-ineligible, and content targeting under-18s can be removed. — [TikTok CG Safety & Civility](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/safety-civility)
- [3P] In Vietnam, authorities have publicly called out influencers for false milk and height-growth claims aimed at parents. This shows enforcement attention on child-related health claims. — [VietnamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/influencers-mislead-with-false-milk-height-growth-claims-says-authority-2431796.html)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Show safety framing ("don't try at home" or "with an adult") on screen. Avoid depicting dangerous demonstrations as easy to copy, and fact-check AI-written scripts, since AI hallucinations are a misinformation risk.

### Gaps
- I didn't fetch YouTube's Harmful or Dangerous Content policy or TikTok's "Dangerous activities and challenges" section verbatim, so the specific wording is not quoted here.

## 7. Copyright: music and sounds

### Takeaway
On YouTube, music added through the Shorts audio tools gets a special claim that is generally harmless. External copyrighted music is subject to standard Content ID claims, blocks or removals. For Shorts over 1 minute, sources conflict on whether any Content ID claim blocks the video. On TikTok, the commercial-sound restrictions for business accounts were not verified here.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Music from outside the Shorts audio library is "eligible for standard Content ID claims and copyright removal requests." — [YouTube Help: Manage Shorts as a rights holder](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13053317)
- [OFFICIAL] Shorts can be up to 3 minutes long (since Oct 2024). — [YouTube Help: Understand three-minute Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en-uk)
- [3P, conflicting] "Shorts over one minute with an active Content ID claim are blocked globally." This may be outdated or partly correct, so verify on the YouTube help page above. — [foximusic](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/youtube-shorts-copyright-music-library-guide/), [subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-copyright-guide)
- [3P] Studio Audio Library tracks are royalty-free and not Content ID-claimed. — [foximusic](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/youtube-shorts-copyright-music-library-guide/)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] For an animated series, use original or AI-generated music you own the rights to, or licensed royalty-free tracks, plus the in-app libraries. If AI music is the "main focus," YouTube requires disclosure, but background music doesn't need it.

### Gaps
- TikTok Commercial Music Library and business-account rules, and the licensing status of in-app sounds when content is exported, were not researched (out of budget).

## 8. Vietnam: Decree 147/2024/ND-CP and the 2025 Advertising Law amendment

### Takeaway
Decree 147 (effective 25 Dec 2024) requires social network accounts to be verified with a Vietnamese mobile number, or a personal ID number if the user has no Vietnamese number. Unverified accounts cannot post, comment or livestream. Accounts for under-16s must be registered by a parent or guardian, who must also supervise them. Separately, the amended Advertising Law (No. 75/2025/QH15, effective 1 Jan 2026) requires creators and KOLs to clearly label paid or sponsored content (e.g., "QC" or "Sponsored") and makes them liable for the ads they carry.

### Cited Findings
- [3P quoting the Decree; Vietnamese bar association journal] Art. 27(3)(e): domestic and cross-border social networks must verify accounts by Vietnamese mobile number, or by personal identification number if the user has none. Unverified accounts after a 90-day grace period "sẽ không được phép hoạt động" (are not allowed to post, comment or share). Art. 27(3)(a): under-16s register through a parent or guardian's information, and the guardian "có trách nhiệm giám sát, quản lý nội dung trẻ em truy cập, đăng tải và chia sẻ" (is responsible for supervising what the child views, posts and shares). — [Luật sư Việt Nam (lsvn.vn)](https://lsvn.vn/phu-huynh-se-quan-ly-hoat-dong-cua-tre-em-tren-mang-xa-hoi-a150228.html), [lsvn.vn on account verification](https://lsvn.vn/chinh-thuc-bat-buoc-xac-thuc-tai-khoan-mang-xa-hoi-bang-so-dien-thoai-a149711.html), [VOV](https://vov.vn/xa-hoi/tu-hom-nay-nguoi-dung-mxh-phai-xac-thuc-moi-duoc-dang-bai-binh-luan-post1144421.vov), [Supreme People's Procuracy news](https://vksndtc.gov.vn/tin-tuc/tin-tong-hop/chinh-thuc-bat-buoc-xac-thuc-tai-khoan-mang-xa-hoi-d8-t12627.html)
- [3P] Some sources cite Art. 23(3)(e), Art. 27(3)(b) and Art. 82(10) as related provisions, and Art. 67 limits gaming time for under-18s. — [hoatieu.vn](https://hoatieu.vn/phap-luat/chinh-thuc-can-xac-thuc-tai-khoan-mang-xa-hoi-bang-so-dien-thoai-226980), [hethongphapluat](https://hethongphapluat.com/phap-luat/node/8571/amp)
- [3P] A draft amendment to Decree 147 (Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism) proposes tighter verification and shorter gaming time for under-16s. It is a proposal only. — [Báo Pháp Luật](https://doanhnhan.baophapluat.vn/de-xuat-rut-ngan-thoi-gian-choi-game-cua-nguoi-duoi-16-tuoi-e1a2c853.html)
- [3P, law firm] Amended Advertising Law 75/2025/QH15, passed 16 Jun 2025 and effective 1 Jan 2026: social media users must use signs to separate advertising or sponsored content from other content. Platforms must provide tools for this. A new class of "advertising conveyors" (people who advertise, recommend or endorse for profit) must verify advertisers and products (Art. 15A rights). — [Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/vietnam-amends-key-aspects-of-advertising-law/), [Vietnam News](https://vietnamnews.vn/economy/1719670/na-passes-revised-advertising-law-to-tighten-accountability-for-online-content.html), [VietnamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/from-2026-influencers-must-verify-products-before-promoting-2468764.html)
- [3P, unverified] A penalty decree, "Decree 87/2026/ND-CP," on advertising violations was mentioned in one search result but not opened. Fine levels are unconfirmed.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] For a Vietnam-based creator, the account must be verified with a Vietnamese phone number, or the account can't post. A kids' science channel has no special Decree 147 creator duty that I found. The child-related duties fall on parents and platforms. Any sponsored episode, such as a toy or education brand, must carry a clear "QC" or "Quảng cáo" (ad) label from 2026. YouTube's paid-promotion checkbox and TikTok's content-disclosure toggle are the natural ways to show it.

### Gaps
- Not verified against the official gazette text (vbpl.vn or chinhphu.vn fetch failed). Article numbers come from lawyer and press summaries. I found no Vietnamese rule requiring AI-content labels for creators as of 2026, though the Digital Technology Industry Law and AI-law developments were not researched.
